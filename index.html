How to use HumanBody 3D

  • Drag to rotate · Scroll to zoom · Right-drag to pan · Click a part to study it.
  • Pick a level at the top: the same 3D body is explained differently for Class 1, Class 6, Class 10, an undergraduate or a PhD student.
  • Toggle systems on the left to add or remove layers — skin, bone, muscle, organs, vessels, nerves.
  • Use the 🌳 tree in the left rail to study one body part at a time instead of the whole body: open a region (Head & Neck, Lower Limb…), click a part, then hit 🛠 on a branch to light up only the layers it needs or 🎯 to be quizzed on that region alone. ☰ List switches back to the flat list.
  • Switch to the 2D plate (top bar, or press P) for a licensed real anatomical reference. Use the Real reference control to choose an interactive projected overlay when you want clickable regions, labels and SVG export. Both views work where WebGL does not.
  • Pick a complexion and switch ♀ ↔ ♂ in the left rail: both change the model's shape, not just the label.
  • Use X-ray to fade the skin, Slice to cut the body open, and Micro view (inside a part, at Undergraduate level or above) to zoom into cells and tissues.
  • Press 🎯 Quiz to test yourself and 🎒 Tour for a guided lesson at your level.
  • On a phone or tablet: one finger rotates the body, two fingers pinch to zoom and pan, and a tap studies a part. The bar along the bottom opens Layers, Tools, Search, Info and More — swipe a sheet down to put it away.
  • Shortcuts: 1–5 level · T tree/list · P 2D/3D · / search · X x-ray · R reset view · Esc close panels.

This atlas combines source-derived anatomical references with educational overlays. It is not patient-specific and not a medical device: always consult a clinician for medical decisions.
